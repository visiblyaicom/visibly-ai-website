## What is answer engine optimization (AEO)?

**Answer engine optimization (AEO)** is the practice of structuring your content so AI-powered answer engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and similar tools — cite it when responding to user questions. Unlike traditional SEO, which optimizes for search engine rankings, AEO optimizes for AI citation.

The distinction matters because AI engines don't return a list of links. They return an answer — and they pull that answer from content that is structured in a specific way.

## How is AEO different from SEO?

AEO and SEO are complementary, not competing. SEO helps your content rank on Google. AEO helps your content get cited by AI engines. You need both.

Traditional SEO focuses on backlinks, keyword density, and domain authority. AEO focuses on content structure: how clearly and directly your content answers a specific question, and whether that answer can be extracted by a machine without reading your entire page.

A page can rank #1 on Google and be completely invisible in ChatGPT answers. The signals that determine AI citation are different from the signals that determine search ranking.

## Why does AEO matter for WordPress bloggers?

AI search is becoming the primary discovery surface for a growing share of internet users. ChatGPT now handles over 1 billion queries per week. Perplexity is growing rapidly. Google AI Overviews appear at the top of results for millions of searches daily.

If your content is not structured for AI citation, you are invisible in that growing layer of search. Traffic from traditional Google results is declining for many content sites. AEO is how you offset that loss.

## What are the 8 signals that determine AI citation?

Based on research across hundreds of content sites, these are the structural signals that most strongly correlate with AI citation:

### 1. Question-format headings

Use H2s phrased as questions — "What is X?" rather than "Overview of X." AI engines are designed to answer questions, so they look for content organized the same way. If your headings are not questions, AI engines have to infer what question your section answers.

### 2. Short direct answers (40–60 words)

Place a direct, complete answer in the first sentence under each question heading. AI engines pull this paragraph directly. The answer should be 40–60 words — long enough to be complete, short enough to be extractable. Do not bury your answer in the middle of a long paragraph.

### 3. FAQ schema markup

**FAQ schema** is structured data that tells AI engines exactly which questions your page answers and what the answers are. AI engines can parse it directly without reading the full page. It is one of the highest-leverage AEO signals you can add.

### 4. Defined terms

Bold key definitions early in the article. Glossary-style content — where you define a term and explain it clearly — gets cited heavily by AI engines. If your content uses jargon without defining it, AI engines are less likely to trust it as an authoritative source.

### 5. Article schema

**Article schema** includes `datePublished`, `author`, and `headline` in your page's structured data. It signals freshness and authority to AI engines. A page with no publication date looks stale. A page with an author signals that a real person with expertise wrote it.

### 6. Word count 800+

Thin content — pages under 800 words — rarely gets cited by AI engines. This is not about padding; it is about depth. A short page usually signals that a topic is not covered thoroughly enough to be a trustworthy source.

### 7. Meta description

Meta descriptions are used by some AI engines as a content summary signal. Write yours as a complete, accurate sentence — not a click-bait hook. AI engines are not trying to get someone to click; they are evaluating whether your page summarizes the topic accurately.

### 8. Heading hierarchy

A clear H1 → H2 → H3 structure helps AI engines parse the document and understand which questions are primary and which are sub-questions. Inconsistent heading structure makes it harder for AI to extract the right answer.

## How do you know if your content is AEO-optimized?

The fastest way is to run your existing posts through an AEO scorer. For WordPress sites, Visibly AI scores each post on all 8 signals in the Gutenberg sidebar as you write — similar to how Yoast shows you an SEO score, but for AI citation signals.

You can install it free from the [WordPress plugin directory](https://wordpress.org/plugins/visibly-ai/). No API key required.

## Can you do AEO and SEO at the same time?

Yes — and you should. The structural changes that improve AEO (question-format headings, direct answers, FAQ schema) also improve how clearly your content communicates its topic to Google. AEO is not a replacement for SEO; it is an additional optimization layer that addresses the AI search layer specifically.

Think of Yoast or Rank Math for traditional SEO, and Visibly AI for the AEO layer on top.

## What is the difference between AEO and GEO?

**Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** is a broader term that covers optimizing content for any generative AI system. AEO is a subset of GEO focused specifically on answer engines — tools that respond to direct questions with cited sources. In practice, the two terms are often used interchangeably.

## Summary

AEO is the practice of structuring your WordPress content so AI answer engines can extract and cite it directly. The 8 core signals are: question-format headings, short direct answers, FAQ schema, defined terms, Article schema, 800+ words, meta description accuracy, and clear heading hierarchy.

As AI search grows, content that is not structured for AI citation will become increasingly invisible. AEO is how you stay visible in the new search landscape.
